## From the Chabadpedia entry "יידיש" (read 2026-11-07; text is GFDL 1.2, so facts only, no copying)
- Yiddish has regional dialects; the entry does NOT say which dialect Chabad speaks. A Gemini answer said "Litvish", but the entry does not confirm it. Still unverified: needs recordings of the Rebbe's farbrengens and a Chabad Yiddish speaker.
- Spelling: YIVO rules (Vilna, 1925) were first rejected by the Haredi world; today most Haredim write almost as YIVO prescribes. Words of Hebrew origin are written as in Hebrew, accepted by both sides (Soviet spelling wrote them phonetically, e.g. שאבעס; do not follow it).
- Rabbeinu Hazaken, the Nesi'im and the Baal Shem Tov said Torah in Yiddish. The Rebbe encouraged Yiddish among Chabad families and stressed that children learn best in the language they understand best. Sources cited there: Likkutei Sichos vol. 21 p. 447 ff; Sicha of Shabbat Vayeishev 5748 (Sefer HaSichos 5748 p. 158).
- Script: Hebrew letters only.

## From the Hebrew Wikipedia entry "ניבים ביידיש" (CC BY-SA 4.0; pasted by the owner 2026-11-08)
- Spoken Yiddish today is Eastern. Two main living dialects: Middle ("Hasidic Yiddish", Poland/West Galicia) and Northeastern = Litvish (Baltics, Belarus, north Poland/Ukraine; today the Old Yishuv in Jerusalem, Beit Shemesh, the Eda HaChareidit, and Lithuanian-descended Haredim). Litvish has many sub-dialects.
- The entry does NOT say what Chabad speaks. Geography (Belarus) fits Litvish; the owner decided to go with Litvish.
- The standard orthography (YIVO) is based mainly on the Northeastern pronunciation, which is why a Litvish base and YIVO-like spelling fit together.
- Verified Litvish forms from the entry's comparison table (Litvish vs Middle/Southeastern): דאָס *dos* (dus), זאָגן *zogn* (zugn), קוגל *kugl* (kigl), קליין *kleyn* (klayn), צוויי *tsvey* (tsvay), ברויט *breyt* (broyt), שטעטל *shtetl* (shteytl). Use these as ground truth for the first pronunciation notes.
