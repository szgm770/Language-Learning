# Spanish for Hebrew speakers – blueprint

Course id `es-for-he`, Latin-American neutral Spanish (`speechLang: es-MX`; *tú* and *ustedes*, no *vosotros*; a tip mentions *vos* and Spain once).
Same rules as English (CLAUDE.md): content YAML only, Torah-observant, lexeme ids unique, learn before practise. Read `en-a1.md` and `en-a2-b2.md` for the lesson pattern.

## Why the method differs from English

| Topic | English for Hebrew speakers | Spanish for Hebrew speakers | What we do |
|-------|------------------------------|------------------------------|------------|
| Spelling ↔ sound | chaotic, A0 needs many sound lessons | almost perfectly phonetic | A0 is short: one letters unit, one sounds unit, one cognates unit. After A0 no pronunciation hints. |
| Vowels | 12+ vowels, long/short | five pure vowels, nearly Hebrew אַ אֶ אִ אוֹ אוּ | one tip, then listening drills |
| Consonants | th, w/v, r | b = v, j = ח, h silent, ll/y, ñ = ני, rr rolled, c/z (seseo) | sounds unit with minimal pairs |
| Gender | none for nouns | masculine / feminine (Hebrew has it too: positive transfer) | every noun is taught **with its article** (*el libro, la casa*); tips use Hebrew gender as a bridge; exceptions (*el día, la mano*) get their own cards |
| Verbs | few endings | an ending for every person (like Hebrew past/future) | conjugation tables in grammar notes, `fill_blank` by person, pronouns often dropped (as in Hebrew) |
| "To be" | one verb | **ser** vs **estar**; Hebrew drops "to be" in the present, so this is the biggest trap | a full unit at A1, revisited at A2 and B1 |
| Past | simple / continuous / perfect | pretérito vs imperfecto; Hebrew has one past | introduced at A2, drilled with story contrasts at B1 |
| Subjunctive | almost gone | everywhere; no Hebrew equivalent | starts at B1 with fixed triggers (*quiero que…*), deepens at B2–C1 |
| Pronouns | simple | clitics (*me lo da*), personal *a* | small steps, many dialogue gaps |
| Typing | plain Latin | á é í ó ú ñ ü ¿ ¡ | on-screen special-letter keys; missing accents are forgiven ("accent" verdict), **ñ is never forgiven** |

Teaching order is therefore "read early, speak early": the first lesson already has full sentences, because Hebrew speakers can read Spanish aloud correctly after one hour.

## Story and characters

Yael (יעל) moves to Mexico City for a year to work at a software company. People: **Mateo** (colleague), **Sofía** (colleague, friendly neighbour), **Don Ricardo** (landlord, widower), **Doña Carmen** (runs the local shop and bakery), **Lucía** (her Spanish teacher), **Dani** (Yael's brother in Israel, appears on calls), **Dr. Herrera**. The Jewish community gives natural scenes: a Friday-night dinner with a host family, a kosher restaurant in Polanco, a community Sunday event. All mixed outings are groups or colleagues; no dating.

## Levels (units of about 6 lessons: new, new, grammar, listening, reading, review)

| Level | Units | Highlights |
|-------|-------|-----------|
| A0 | 3 | letras, sonidos, palabras que ya conoces (cognates) → `a0-test` |
| A1 | 10 | greetings, *ser*, family, food and numbers, the home, *-ar / -er / -ir*, daily routine, the city, *gustar* → `a1-test` |
| A2 | 10 | *ser/estar*, *tener* idioms, *querer/poder*, reflexive verbs, shopping, health, *ir a*, comparatives, first past tenses → `a2-test` |
| B1 | 10 | pretérito vs imperfecto, present perfect, future and conditional, object pronouns, *por/para*, commands, first subjunctive → `b1-test` |
| B2 | 10 | subjunctive in depth, *si*-clauses, passive and *se*, reported speech, pluperfect, connectors → `b2-test` |
| C1 | 6 | past subjunctive nuances, register, idioms, academic Spanish → `c1-test` |
| Packs | 6+ | word-builder units like English `v1–v6`, plus B1+ free-writing tasks |
